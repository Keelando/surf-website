About Lightstation Reports

BC's staffed lightstations report wind, sea state, swell and visibility from remote coastal locations, observed and phoned in by the lightkeepers on site. Reporting schedules vary by station; each card shows its own under Station Details.

Environment Canada is the authoritative source for marine decisions. This page displays their bulletins, and a report can be late, missed, or absent from our copy. Check EC directly before going out:

EC Lightstation Reports ↗ EC Marine Weather ↗
How the reporting schedule works

The observations reach us in two different Environment Canada bulletins. FPCN61 is written out in full, covers every station, and is issued at ten past the hour on the 00/03/06/09/12/15/18/21 UTC cycle. The SXCN bulletins are terse and coded, cover regional subsets, and are issued at thirty or forty past (the north coast on the half hour, the rest ten minutes later) on the 02/05/08/11/14/17/20/23 UTC cycle. Most stations appear in only one of them, so they report roughly every three hours. EC's own Lightstation Reports page ↗ shows the coded bulletins, so a reading there can be timed a little differently from the same reading here. FPCN61 has no web page of its own. It is a raw bulletin on the MSC Datamart ↗, filed under a new path every hour.

Nine stations appear in both bulletins, Merry Island and Trial Island among them. The lightkeeper takes one observation and it goes out in both formats, so we store it once rather than twice. The coded bulletin sets the time, because it states the minute the reading was taken; the written-out one only names a rounded hour. Those stations report on the same three-hourly rhythm as the rest, and the two formats together fill in more of each reading than either carries alone.

Not every station runs a full cycle. Cape Mudge and Pulteney Point report four times a day, in daylight hours, with a long overnight silence. And no station has published the 08:40 or 09:10 UTC slot in the history we hold, so every cycle has an overnight gap and none reaches the nominal eight reports a day.

The schedule shown for each station is measured from its own recent reports rather than declared in advance, so it follows what a station actually does. A station that has not reported often enough for a pattern to emerge is labelled as irregular instead.
